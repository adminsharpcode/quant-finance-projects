First-Principles Mathematical Specification

Theoretical derivations driving the pricing kernels, lattice boundaries, and volatility surfaces

1. Continuous-Time Black-76 Martingale Equation

Under the risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$, the futures price $F_t$ requires zero initial capital due to daily mark-to-market margins. Consequently, $F_t$ is a martingale with identically zero drift:

dF_t = \sigma F_t dW_t^\mathbb{Q} \implies C = e^{-rT} [F \Phi(d_1) - K \Phi(d_2)], \quad P = e^{-rT} [K \Phi(-d_2) - F \Phi(-d_1)]

2. Cox-Ross-Rubinstein Lattice & Snell Envelope

American futures options permit early exercise at any stopping time $\tau \in [0, T]$. Backward induction solves the dynamic programming Bellman equation:

V_{i,j} = \max\left( \text{Intrinsic}(F_{i,j}), \; e^{-r\Delta t} [p V_{i+1, j+1} + (1-p) V_{i+1, j}] \right) \quad \text{where } p = \frac{1-d}{u-d}

3. Gatheral's SVI Surface & Arbitrage Constraints

Total implied variance $w(k) = \sigma^2(k) T$ as a function of log-moneyness $k = \ln(K/F)$ satisfies:

w(k) = a + b \left( \rho (k - m) + \sqrt{(k - m)^2 + \sigma^2} \right)

To prevent butterfly arbitrage, the risk-neutral density $g(k) \ge 0$ must hold everywhere:

g(k) = \left( 1 - \frac{k w'(k)}{2 w(k)} \right)^2 - \frac{w'(k)^2}{4} \left( \frac{1}{w(k)} + \frac{1}{4} \right) + \frac{w''(k)}{2} \ge 0
